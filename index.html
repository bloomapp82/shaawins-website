The 5 errors we find most on Brooklyn credit reports

We've reviewed hundreds of reports from Liberty Avenue to Flatbush. The same five mistakes keep showing up — and all five are disputable.

1. Accounts that aren't yours

Mixed files happen when a bureau merges you with someone who shares your name or a similar Social Security number. It's more common than you'd think in dense neighborhoods with many families sharing surnames. The fix is a dispute with proof of identity — and we insist on written confirmation of the correction.

2. Paid collections still showing a balance

You paid, the collector never updated the bureaus. Under the FCRA the entry must be accurate; a paid account showing a balance is not. These are among the quickest wins.

3. Late payments that were on time

A single 30-day late can cost 60–100 points. When your statements show the payment cleared before the due date, the late mark has to go.

4. Re-aged debt

Some collectors "refresh" the date of first delinquency so an old debt looks new and stays on your report longer. That's illegal, and it's one of the first things we check.

5. Duplicate entries

The same debt listed by the original creditor and two collectors — three hits for one problem. Only one can stand.

Glossary

Utilization
Balances divided by limits, per card and overall. The second-biggest factor in your score after payment history.
Hard inquiry
A lender checking your report because you applied. Small, temporary dip; stays on the report two years.
Charge-off
A creditor writing off a debt as unlikely to be paid, usually after 180 days late. You still owe it.
Collection
A debt sold or assigned to a collection agency. Appears as a separate account.
Date of first delinquency
When you first fell behind. Most negative items drop off seven years after this date — not from the last payment.
Pay for delete
Paying a collector in exchange for removing the entry. Not all collectors agree; get it in writing first.
Goodwill letter
Asking a creditor to remove an accurate late mark as a courtesy, usually after a long on-time history.
Secured card
A card backed by a cash deposit. The safest way to build or rebuild history.
